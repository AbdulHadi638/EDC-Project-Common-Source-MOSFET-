Introduction:  
In this project, a single-stage common source amplifier is used with a MOSFET.  

A 3 inch by 3 inch PCB is used.  

The PCB is designed, then etched and drilled.  

After that, components are placed and soldered on the board.  

The board includes a MOSFET, load and source resistors according to calculations, coupling and bypass capacitors, and input/output connectors.  

Most parts are placed compactly so that the signal flows clearly without noise.  

After soldering, the working amplifier circuit was tested.  

A 24 mV peak-to-peak sine wave with a frequency of 50 Hz was applied.  

Then an 18V supply was provided.  

To measure different voltages, probes were placed at various points on the circuit.  

The final circuit is a small and neatly wired board used for lab measurement.

Observation:  
Simulation results are very close to practical results.

---

Discussion:  
The circuit behaved as expected for a common source amplifier.  

The simulation and practical results matched closely.  

After proper bias adjustment, the MOSFET operating point became stable.  

The output signal showed clear amplification with a 180 degree phase shift.  

The voltage gain achieved was approximately 34 V/V, which is within the expected range.  

---

Conclusion:  
The project was successfully completed.  

The common source amplifier worked as expected after construction and testing.  

Initial issues included loose PCB connections and unstable oscilloscope probe contacts.  

These issues caused noise in the output signal.  

After fixing the connections and using properly secured probes, the signal became stable.  

Overall, the final results matched the theoretical and simulated expectations.  

The project helped in understanding MOSFET biasing, amplifier operation, and PCB implementation in a practical way.
